Primary publication: Han Li and Gregory A. Margulis, *Effective estimates on
integral quadratic forms: Masser's conjecture, generators of orthogonal groups,
and bounds in reduction theory*, GAFA **26** (2016), 874–908,
DOI: 10.1007/s00039-016-0379-2.

Author-hosted primary PDF:
https://li-han.weebly.com/uploads/5/1/3/3/51338781/equivalence.pdf

This record is a source transcription and separate arithmetic derivation,
not a value calculated from the port implementation. The equation (3)
exponent was inspected in the rendered original PDF, printed page 2
(PDF page index 1), rather than inferred from OCR or a previous test.

## Theorem 1, equation (3), printed page 2

Assumptions: n>=3; symmetric nonsingular integral n-by-n matrices A, B;
an integral equivalence A = gamma_0^t B gamma_0 with gamma_0 in GL_n(Z).

Equation (3) prints the determinant factor with no leading minus before its exponent.

\[
\|\gamma\| < C_n |\det B|^{(13n^3-39n^2+36n+52)/(20n)}
                 \|A\|^{(13n^3-13n^2+52n+20)/40}
                 \|B\|^{(13n^3-13n^2+72n-20)/40}.
\]

At n=3, the three exponents are respectively 8/3, 41/4, and 43/4.
Rounding them up for integer bases at least one gives 3, 11, 11.
For A=B=diag(2,-2,6) the corresponding strict integer majorant
with C_3=1 is 24^3 * 6^22 + 1.

## Theorem 2, printed page 3

For n>=3 and a nonsingular integral quadratic form B, its integral
orthogonal group is generated by matrices of size strictly less than
C_n * |det B|^(n^6) * ||B||^(3n^4).

## Lemma 20, equation (31), printed page 18

For n>m, nonsingular integral A,B and a primitive integral embedding
A=tau_0^t B tau_0, a unimodular completion exists with Gram norm
bounded by a dimension constant times
|det A|^(n-m) * |det B| + ||A||. Theorem 1 applies to the completed form.

## Source consistency caveat

The proof of Theorem 4, section 8.5, prints a NEGATIVE determinant
exponent in one intermediate use of Theorem 1. This differs from
Theorem 1, equation (3), on page 2. Tests invoking the *stated*
Theorem 1 (3) may use the printed positive exponent as a conservative
bound. Any assertion of the sharper negative bound must reconcile the
discrepancy independently rather than silently choosing a sign.
