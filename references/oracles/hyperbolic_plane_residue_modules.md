# Independent finite-module oracle for the hyperbolic plane

Let U have Gram matrix [[0,1],[1,0]] over Z. For an integral matrix
[[a,b],[c,d]], preservation of U gives ac=bd=0 and ad+bc=1.
Thus O(U) consists exactly of I, -I, the coordinate swap S, and -S.

Modulo 4, the cyclic additive submodules generated by (2,0), (0,2),
and (2,2) each have order two. The swap S carries (2,0) to (0,2).
Every member of O(U) sends (2,2) to itself modulo 4, whereas none
sends (2,2) to (2,0). No group element can carry an order-two
submodule to the order-four submodule generated by (1,0), because
invertible linear maps preserve subgroup cardinality.

The calculations use only the displayed Gram-preservation equations
and coordinate reduction; no port implementation is involved.

For nested flags at level four, let F be the ordered pair of submodules
<(2,0)> <= <(1,0)> and let F' be <(0,2)> <= <(0,1)>.
The swap S transports F to F'. By contrast the flag
<(2,2)> <= <(1,1)> cannot transport to F: its first stage is
fixed by all four integral orthogonal matrices and is distinct from
<(2,0)>. This remains an obstruction even though the corresponding
stages have equal cardinalities two and four.

For labelled finite subsets at level four, the same swap carries the
red singleton {(1,0)} and blue singleton {(2,0)} to the red singleton
{(0,1)} and blue singleton {(0,2)}. It cannot carry the red singleton
{(1,0)} to {(1,1)} because the four integral isometries send (1,0)
only to (1,0), (3,0), (0,1), (0,3) modulo four. Colour labels cannot
be permuted.

For simultaneous marked data at level four, take the source vector
(1,0), source cyclic submodule <(2,0)>, and red singleton {(1,0)}.
The coordinate swap transports all three simultaneously to (0,1),
<(0,2)> and {(0,1)}. Replacing only the target red singleton by
{(1,0)} makes simultaneous transport impossible: the unique one of
the four isometries sending (1,0) to (0,1) is S, and S sends the red
singleton to {(0,1)}.

For simultaneous *different-level* transport, consider a source vector
(1,0) modulo 2 and a source vector (1,0) modulo 4. Requiring targets
(0,1) at both levels is achieved by S. Requiring (0,1) modulo 2 but
(1,0) modulo 4 is impossible: the latter condition forces I as the
unique isometry among I,-I,S,-S, whereas I does not swap the vector
modulo 2. This proves a nonempty local transporter set at each level
can have empty intersection in the integral group.

For full-rank sublattices containing 4Z^2, set
M = 2Z e + 4Z f and N = 4Z e + 2Z f. The coordinate swap
transports M to N. The sublattice P generated by (2,2) and (0,4)
has index eight, like M, but is fixed by every member of O(U):
sign changes and swapping coordinates preserve the congruence
description P = {(x,y): x,y even and x=y modulo 4}.
Consequently P cannot transport to M, since (2,0) belongs to M
but not P. All three sublattices contain 4Z^2.

For the positive definite standard rank-three lattice I_3, an integral
orthogonal matrix has columns of Euclidean norm one, hence each column
is a signed coordinate vector. Orthogonality forces distinct positions:
O(I_3) is the signed permutation group of order 2^3 * 3! = 48.
Reduction modulo 3 is injective on this group. The subgroup H generated
by the transposition of the first two coordinates has order two and
index 24. It is nonnormal, since conjugating by the transposition of
coordinates two and three gives a different transposition. The preimage
of its level-three reduction is exactly H, with no additional elements.
Inside O(I_3), the coordinate permutation subgroup S_3 has order six.
Its reduction modulo three is also injective, and the same transposition
subgroup H has index three in this explicitly generated ambient subgroup.
